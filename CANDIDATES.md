# Candidates — The Funniest Office Joke, Judged Head to Head

Ten one-line office jokes generated with DeepSeek (100milionseek,
deepseek-v4.1-flash-free) as second brain, two generation calls of five
each. DeepSeek's first critique attempt failed outright (provider
502/503/empty across 3 retries); the retry answered but its reply was
truncated mid-ranking (only #1 visible: joke 8, 9/10). A follow-up
head-to-head micro-call also answered truncated but with a clear verdict
("B wins"). The ranking below is the builder's own critique against the
kit's judge criteria, in the judge's order, informed by DeepSeek's two
verdicts (both picked #8):

1. Real joke with a setup and a turn, about office life.
2. Turns on something anyone who has worked in an office knows.
3. Surprises — a turn the reader does not see coming, not a plain complaint.
4. Lands in one line, every word earning its place, punchline last.
5. Feels new, not a joke already going round the office.

## The candidates

1. **The consultant hired to fix our reply-all problem emailed his findings to the whole company, and eleven people replied asking to be removed from the thread.** (163 chars)
   Strong irony, setup + turn, universal reply-all pain. Weakness: the punchline is half-spent once "emailed his findings to the whole company" lands; long.
2. **I blocked two hours for deep work, and Outlook instantly booked a meeting in it titled "Why Nothing Gets Done."** (111 chars) — RUNNER-UP
   Setup (blocked deep work) + turn (a meeting booked *inside* it); the calendar auto-booking over focus time is universal; the surprise is the meeting's own title naming the complaint; one tight line, punchline last; a fresh angle on the meeting trope rather than the plain "could have been an email" complaint. Lost the head-to-head duel to #8: its turn is single, while #8's trap backfires on its own author.
3. **Standup ran forty minutes, so we scheduled a follow-up standup about why standups run long, which ran forty minutes.** (125 chars)
   Neat recursion, universal standup bloat. Weakness: the turn is predictable once "follow-up standup" appears; the joke tells you its own mechanism.
4. **HR ran an anonymous morale survey and presented the results as "Feedback We Can All Learn From," with quotes and job titles attached.** (139 chars)
   Good punchline ("quotes and job titles attached"). Weakness: reads slightly like a complaint about HR rather than a joke with a turn; the setup is long.
5. **I opened with "per my last email," she replied "per my last email," and now we're two people politely threatening each other in writing.** (141 chars)
   Fresh, relatable, and the "politely threatening each other" turn lands. Weakness: the turn is more of a summary than a surprise.
6. **My out-of-office promised I'd return with "limited email access" — two days back, and the limit is holding at zero.** (118 chars)
   Clever self-own. Weakness: the turn ("holding at zero") is a small wordplay payoff, and out-of-office jokes are a crowded lane.
7. **"Quick question," she typed, then sent a 40-minute screen recording and a calendar invite for the follow-up.** (111 chars)
   Very recognizable. Weakness: mostly a complaint stated with an example; the turn is expected once "40-minute screen recording" arrives.
8. **Someone kept stealing my lunch, so I labeled today's "Do not eat — stool sample." It worked, but now I don't want it either.** (124 chars) — **PICKED**
   Good self-own turn. The only candidate with a genuine second turn: the trap works *and* punishes the trapper. Fridge theft is universal office life; the surprise is that the revenge costs you your own lunch; one line, punchline last; fresh angle, not the tired meeting/printer complaint. DeepSeek's ranking (once it answered) put this #1 at 9/10, and its head-to-head duel picked it over #2.
9. **I spent 20 minutes solving calendar Tetris for eight people, and the meeting lasted four minutes.** (97 chars)
   Tight contrast, universal. Weakness: the turn is arithmetic (20 min vs 4 min) rather than a surprise; it states the absurdity instead of turning on it.
10. **He unmuted after 30 minutes of silence to say "sorry, go ahead," then muted again — having said his piece.** (110 chars)
    Very recognizable and the "having said his piece" close is neat. Weakness: gentle rather than laugh-out-loud; the turn is a wry observation, not a surprise.

## Pick rationale

#8 wins. It is the only candidate with a genuine second turn: the trap
works *and* punishes the trapper, so the revenge costs you your own
lunch. That double turn is the strongest surprise in the set, fridge
theft is universal office life, and the line is tight (124 chars) with
the punchline last. DeepSeek's ranking put it #1 (9/10) and its
head-to-head duel picked it over the runner-up #2. The Outlook joke
(#2) was the builder's first pick for its tight single turn, but it
lost the duel: one turn against two, and its punchline (the meeting
title) is telegraphed by its middle, while #8's second turn lands
only in the final words.
